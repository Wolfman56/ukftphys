## Abstract

We present the simulation of the **Void Scalar** ("The Ripple")—the emergent mechanism for Dark Energy in the framework of Entropic Unification [1]. In a discrete causal graph, "vacuum" is defined by low information density, but a minimum connectivity threshold is required for the graph to exist at all.

Lattice simulations (Experiment 47) demonstrate that as the vacuum density constraint ($\epsilon \to 0$) is approached, the system's internal tension does **not** vanish. Instead, it hits a hard "Vacuum Floor" at a finite positive pressure ($\sim 0.2$ energy units). This residual tension manifests physically as a **Cosmological Constant ($\Lambda$)**, driving the accelerated expansion of low-density regions.

## 1. Introduction: The Cosmological Constant Problem

Generations of physicists have struggled with the massive discrepancy between the predicted vacuum energy of Quantum Field Theory ($10^{120}$ Planck Density) and the observed Dark Energy ($10^{-120}$ Planck Density). This "Cosmological Constant Problem" suggests a fundamental misunderstanding of the vacuum state.

The **Entropic Unification** framework [1] posits that spacetime is not a continuous manifold but a discrete, evolving graph of causal relations. In this view, "energy" is the cost of manipulating graph connectivity. The vacuum is not "empty space" but a low-density information network.

We propose that Dark Energy is the **structural yield stress** of this network—the minimum energy required to prevent it from becoming causally disconnected.

## 2. Methodology: Simulating the Vacuum Floor

We model the vacuum as a scalar field $\phi$ on a deformable lattice, subject to an "Existence Constraint."

- **Objective Function**: Minimize the Global Tension (Gradient Energy):
  $$ E = \int (\nabla \phi)^2 dV $$
- **Constraint**: The local field amplitude must remain above a minimum threshold $\epsilon$ to represent a connected node:
  $$ |\phi| \ge \epsilon $$
- **Simulation**: We sweep the constraint parameter $\epsilon$ from **1.0 (Matter Density)** down to **0.001 (Deep Void)** using a Metropolis-Hastings algorithm.
- **Measurement**: We measure the resulting **Vacuum Tension** (Average Gradient Energy Density) $P(\epsilon)$.

## 3. Results: The "Vacuum Floor" Discovery

**Experiment 47** revealed a critical phase transition in the vacuum behavior.

As we lowered the density constraint $\epsilon$, the tension initially dropped (representing gravitational binding potential). However, at low densities ($\epsilon < 0.1$), the tension plateaued rather than vanishing.

| Epsilon (Density $\epsilon$) | Vacuum Tension (Pressure $P$) | Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1.00** (High) | 1.95 | **Matter / Gravity** |
| 0.50 | 1.08 | Transition |
| 0.10 | 0.27 | Halo / Void Boundary |
| **0.01** (Low) | **0.20** | **Dark Energy Floor** |
| **0.001** (Vacuum) | **0.20** | **Constant Limit** |

The system exhibits a **Vacuum Floor** at $P \approx 0.20$. This residual tension persists even as the density constraint approaches zero.

## 4. Physical Interpretation

### 4.1 Emergent Dark Energy
This non-zero pressure floor confirms that a discrete causal graph cannot have zero energy. The requirement to maintain global connectivity imposes a minimum cost per unit volume.

In General Relativity, a constant positive vacuum energy density acts as a repulsive force ($\Lambda > 0$). Our simulation shows exactly this: a constant positive pressure in empty space.

### 4.2 The "Ripple" Scalar
We identify this phenomenon with a scalar field fluctuation—the **Void Scalar** or "The Ripple." It is not a particle in the traditional sense, but a coherent, low-amplitude vibration of the entire causal fabric.

### 4.3 Resolution of the Cosmological Constant Problem
The simulation suggests that the "natural" value of the vacuum energy is not zero, but a finite floor determined by the graph's connectivity topology. The vast discrepancy ($10^{120}$) arises because we typically compare this floor to the Planck scale (the graph's resolution limit) rather than the Horizon scale (the graph's connectivity limit).
